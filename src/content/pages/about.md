Hi. I'm Umair.

I build AI systems that companies actually run. Not demos. The kind that a nurse, a planner or a fraud analyst opens on a Monday and depends on by Friday.

By day I'm a Principal AI Engineer at [Zazmic](https://zazmic.ai), a Google Cloud Premier Partner, where I lead and mentor the ML engineers and still write a lot of the code myself. We build multi-agent systems on Google ADK, the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform and Claude for clients in media, healthcare, finance and retail supply chains. Some of those clients are teams at Apple and Meta. Most of them you have never heard of, which is where the interesting problems live.

## How I got here

I grew up in Pakistan and did my master's in data science on a full scholarship, graduating with a gold medal and a thesis about pulling structured data out of messy documents. That thesis turned into my first real production system, and the pattern stuck. Every job since has started with a domain I did not know.

Supply chain at Algo. I did not know what demand planning was. Within months my forecast engine was running for retail clients.

Medical claims at Developers Inc. I had never read an ICD-10 code. The system I led ended up processing 50,000 claims a day.

Code security with Qwiet AI. Graph neural networks over code, then agents that write and verify patches.

The domain is always new. The ability to figure it out is not.

## Why this site exists

In 2025 the ground under AI engineering shifted. Models stopped being the hard part. Tool contracts, evaluation, cost per task and trust became the work. I wanted a place to think about that out loud, with sources, every week, before the thinking hardened into slides.

So every Sunday I write a short note on what changed that week and what I make of it from inside client projects. Most Wednesdays I publish a longer piece on something I built or broke. Everything cites its sources with dates.

## Outside the terminal

Houston, Texas, since 2026, after years between Islamabad and remote US teams. I run, I am learning German slowly, and I have opinions about biryani that I will defend longer than any architecture decision.
