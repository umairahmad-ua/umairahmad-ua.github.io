## Effort as a dial

[Claude Opus 4.7](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/whats-new/2026-w16) landed on Thursday with an xhigh effort level and a feature called Routines in Claude Code. The effort dial is the part I care about. For a year the choice was which model to call. Now the choice is which model and how hard it should think. For our cloud operations agent that is the right control. Reading an alert needs low effort. Proposing a remediation on a production database needs the maximum. Same model, two costs, one config line. I spent Friday mapping our agent steps to effort levels and cut the estimated monthly bill by about a third on paper. I will report the real number when we have a month of data.

Routines let a Claude Code task run on a schedule. I set one up to draft this note from the week's timeline every Saturday. I still write the opinion by hand. The draft saves me twenty minutes. That is the honest size of the win.

## OpenAI puts the sandbox in the SDK

OpenAI described [the next evolution of the Agents SDK](https://openai.com/index/the-next-evolution-of-the-agents-sdk/) on Wednesday. The agent gets a native sandbox and the harness is built into the model layer. This is the third vendor in two weeks to say the harness is their job, after Anthropic's Managed Agents and Google's Agent Engine. I do not think three vendors are wrong at once. I do think enterprise clients will ask where the sandbox runs and who can read the state. Our healthcare clients ask that about every managed service. The answer decides the deal more than the benchmark does.

## Qwen keeps the open end alive

[Qwen3.6-35B-A3B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B) is open weights with three billion active parameters. That is a routing-tier model. I added it to the same eval slot where Mistral Small 4 is running. Results next week if the harness cooperates.
