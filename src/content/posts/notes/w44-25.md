A quieter week on the model side, which gave me time to think about the tools my team actually uses.

## What happened

- MiniMax released [M2](https://www.minimax.io/news/minimax-m2) with open weights, pitched at agentic and coding tasks. Another Chinese lab, another capable open model.
- OpenAI [completed its recapitalization](https://openai.com/index/built-to-benefit-everyone/). The nonprofit becomes the OpenAI Foundation and the Microsoft agreement was rewritten. I will leave the governance analysis to others.
- Cursor released [2.0](https://cursor.com/blog/2-0) with Composer, its own in-house coding model. A tool company training its own model is a bet that the harness matters more than the base model.

## What I make of it

The Cursor move is the one I keep coming back to. Two years ago the model was everything. Now a well-funded editor decides it is cheaper to train a decent model than to depend on someone else's. That only makes sense if most of the value is in the loop around the model, the context management, the tool calls, the retries. I believe that is true. It is the whole reason we invest in eval harnesses and tool contracts instead of prompt tricks.

## The open-model question from clients

Three clients asked me some version of "should we run an open model" this month. My answer is always the same. Run the eval. On extraction and classification, open models often pass. On long multi-step planning with tool use, they usually do not, yet. The gap closes every quarter. The eval set is what tells you when it has closed for your task. Without one you are choosing on vibes.

We are also finishing the first version of our migration agent this week. It translates legacy stored procedures to BigQuery SQL and then reconciles row counts against the source. The translation part took a week. The reconciliation part is taking a month. That ratio is normal.
