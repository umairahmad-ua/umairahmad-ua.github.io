No model releases this week. The lab heads were at [Davos](https://fortune.com/2026/01/23/deepmind-demis-hassabis-anthropic-dario-amodei-yann-lecun-ai-davos/) making predictions. Amodei talked about Nobel-level science from models. Meta confirmed it has internal models in progress. I read the coverage and got back to work. Predictions do not change my sprint.

## Batch three

The migration track moved its third batch of stored procedures this week. Three batches in, the pattern is clear. The translation agent gets about nine in ten procedures right on the first pass. The tenth one is always a procedure with vendor-specific date math or a cursor loop. The validation agent catches those every time so far. That is the number I care about. Not the translation accuracy. The catch rate.

## What broke

One procedure passed reconciliation and was still wrong. Counts matched. Sums matched. The join order had changed and a small set of rows moved between two output tables. Totals were identical across both tables combined. I added a per-table check to the validation agent and a sampling step that pulls fifty random keys and compares them row by row. The finance analyst who found it was not impressed with me. She was right.

## A rule I am adopting

Every reconciliation report now includes what it did not check. If the agent only compared aggregates, it says so. If it sampled, it says how many rows. The analyst reads that section first. A report that names its own blind spots gets trusted faster than a report that claims everything passed.

## On the Davos talk

I do not doubt models will do science. I doubt it matters to the client who needs their month-end close to work on BigQuery by March. Those are different problems on different timelines. I work on the second one.
