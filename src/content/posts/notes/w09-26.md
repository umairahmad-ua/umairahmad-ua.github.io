## The image model

Google released [Nano Banana 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_(language_model)) on Thursday, the image generation model built on Gemini 3.1 Flash. My media clients will care. The Let's Forage team asked about it within the hour. I have a generation agent scoped from January. This moves it closer to a build.

## The designation

On Friday the [US Department of Defense designated Anthropic a supply-chain risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_(language_model)). I am not going to comment on the politics. I am going to comment on what it does to procurement conversations, because I sit in those conversations.

Every enterprise client I work with has a vendor risk process. Until this week, model vendors moved through that process the way any SaaS vendor does. A government designation changes the checklist. Some clients with federal exposure will pause. Others will not care. A few will use it as a reason to demand a multi-vendor architecture they should have wanted anyway.

## What I tell clients

I have never built a client system on a single model vendor. Not because I predicted this. Because models change, prices change, and rate limits change. The Scout system runs on Gemini with Claude as a fallback for specific agents. The migration track benches three models for the translation job. Swapping a vendor is a config change and an eval run, not a rewrite.

That was a good engineering practice last month. This month it is a risk control that a procurement officer can point to. Same architecture, different justification.

## What I make of it

Model choice is now a governance question, not only a technical one. I expect more of this, not less. The way to be ready is boring. Abstract the model behind a contract. Keep the eval suite vendor-neutral. Know your fallback before you need it. I did those things for engineering reasons. I am glad I did.
