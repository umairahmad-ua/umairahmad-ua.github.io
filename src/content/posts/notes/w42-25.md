## Three clouds, three agent runtimes

[Amazon Bedrock AgentCore](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2025/10/amazon-bedrock-agentcore-available) reached general availability on Monday. That makes three managed agent runtimes I now have to understand. Vertex AI Agent Engine on Google Cloud, AgentCore on AWS, and whatever OpenAI is assembling out of AgentKit. They all solve the same problems. Sessions, memory, tool gateways, identity, observability.

I built our agents on Agent Engine because our clients are on Google Cloud. The design choices I made there, typed tool contracts and session state kept outside the model, would move to AgentCore with modest effort. That portability was not an accident.

## Haiku 4.5

Anthropic released [Claude Haiku 4.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_(language_model)) on Wednesday. Small models are where the money is in agent systems. Most steps in a multi-agent workflow are routing, classification, or formatting. Those do not need a frontier model. On the medical claims system I led at Developers Inc, the expensive model touched maybe one in five claims. Everything else went through smaller, cheaper components. The same split applies to agents. I want the orchestrator to be smart and the workers to be cheap.

## Agent Skills

The day after Haiku, Anthropic introduced [Agent Skills](https://thenewstack.io/agent-skills-anthropics-next-bid-to-define-ai-standards/). A skill is a folder of instructions and scripts that an agent loads when the task calls for it. This is a good idea. It is also what we already do by hand with prompt files per domain agent in Scout. Having a shared format for it means less bespoke plumbing.

Google also released [Veo 3.1](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/15/google-releases-veo-3-1-adds-it-to-flow-video-editor/). Our media client will ask about it. My answer will be to wait for the eval.
