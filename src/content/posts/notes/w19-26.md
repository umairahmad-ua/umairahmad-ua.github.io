## Proactive is the new word

[Code with Claude](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/6/code-w-claude-2026/) ran in San Francisco on Wednesday. I followed Simon Willison's notes rather than the stream. The theme was agents that act without being asked. A workflow watches for a condition and starts work. [Managed Agents got three new features](https://9to5mac.com/2026/05/07/anthropic-updates-claude-managed-agents-with-three-new-features/) the next day along the same line.

I have one proactive agent in production. It is the cloud operations agent on Claude. It reads alerts, pulls logs and metrics through MCP tools, and proposes a fix. It does not execute anything outside a pre-approved playbook, and anything destructive waits for a human. Building that permission model took longer than building the agent. The vendors are now selling proactive as a feature. I would sell it as a liability you take on deliberately.

Here is the test I use. If the agent acts and is wrong, who finds out and when? For the operations agent the answer is the on-call engineer, within minutes, because every action posts to the incident channel. For a marketing agent at Let's Forage the answer could be a client's customer, days later. So Scout does not act on its own. It drafts and a person sends.

## The default shifts under everyone

OpenAI made [GPT-5.5 Instant the ChatGPT default](https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-5-instant/) on Tuesday. Most people in most companies will never pick a model. They get the default. That is also true inside our agents. Whatever we set as the model for a specialist is what runs ten thousand times a month. I review those defaults once a quarter and it always turns up one agent still on a model from two generations ago. It worked, so nobody touched it. That is fine until the price or the deprecation notice arrives.

## Small note on the team

We onboarded an engineer this week who had only built agents with LangGraph. Her first ADK agent took two days. The concepts moved over. The names did not. That is the state of the field. Same ideas, different nouns, every vendor.
