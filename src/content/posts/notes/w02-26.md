The year opened with hardware and healthcare.

## What happened

- NVIDIA used its [CES keynote](https://www.axios.com/2026/01/05/nvidia-ces-2026-jensen-huang-speech-ai) on Monday to show the Vera Rubin platform. More compute, on schedule.
- OpenAI launched [ChatGPT Health](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-health/) on Wednesday, a consumer product for medical questions with record connections.
- Anthropic launched [Claude for Healthcare](https://fortune.com/2026/01/11/anthropic-unveils-claude-for-healthcare-and-expands-life-science-features-partners-with-healthex-to-let-users-connect-medical-records/) today at the JPM conference, aimed at providers and life sciences.

## What I make of it

I spent two years at Developers Inc building a claims pipeline that handles 50K claims a day. It cut rejection rates by 35 percent. The model was the easy part of that project. The hard part was the payer rules. Each payer had its own edge cases, its own codes, and its own idea of what a valid claim looked like. We encoded that in a rule engine that sat next to the model, not inside it.

Both launches this week are model-first. They put a general model in front of health data and add connectors. That will work for questions. It will not work for claims, billing, or prior authorization without the rule layer. Somebody has to build that layer per payer and keep it current. The labs will not do it. Partners will.

That is the opening I see for the work I do. The frontier gives you a model that reads medical text well. The value is in the boring integration around it: the codes, the audit trail, the human review queue. I built that once. The demand for it just went up.

The Rubin news matters less to me week to week. Cheaper inference shows up in my cost per task about a year after the keynote. I note it and move on.
