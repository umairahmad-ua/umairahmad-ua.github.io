The headline number this week was 10 gigawatts. [OpenAI and NVIDIA](https://openai.com/index/openai-nvidia-systems-partnership/) signed a letter of intent for that much compute, with up to 100 billion dollars behind it. I do not have an opinion on the financing. I do have an opinion on what it means for the people I build for. Inference is going to keep getting cheaper for a long time. Plan for that.

## Qwen3-Max

Alibaba released [Qwen3-Max](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/qwen3-max-just-scale-it_602621) at over a trillion parameters, and open-sourced Qwen3-VL and Qwen3-Omni alongside it. The open models keep closing the gap on the tasks that matter to my clients. Extraction, classification, structured output. For those, I now test an open model first and reach for a frontier model only when the eval says I have to.

## Claude inside Copilot

Microsoft [added Claude Sonnet 4 and Opus 4.1](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/blog/2025/09/24/expanding-model-choice-in-microsoft-365-copilot/) to Microsoft 365 Copilot. A year ago that would have been unthinkable. Today it is a menu option. Model choice inside a single product is becoming normal, and it should be. Our own agents on Google Cloud already route between Gemini and Claude depending on the task. Clients care about the answer and the invoice. They do not care about the logo.

OpenAI also launched [ChatGPT Pulse](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-pulse/), a proactive daily briefing. Proactive agents are a UX problem more than a model problem. The hard part is knowing when to stay quiet.

## This week at work

We finished the first eval set for the Scout data-analysis agent. Forty questions a marketing analyst would actually ask, with graded answers. It took longer to write the questions than to build the agent. That is the right ratio.
