## Two open models

Z.ai released [GLM-5](https://huggingface.co/blog/mlabonne/glm-5) on Wednesday, a 744B mixture of experts. MiniMax followed with [M2.5](https://www.minimax.io/news/minimax-m25) on Thursday. That makes four large open-weight releases from Chinese labs since late January.

I get asked about open models on almost every client call now. The question is usually framed as cost. My answer is usually about control. An open model lets you pin a version forever. A hosted model changes under you. For the migration track, I want the translation agent to behave the same way in batch twelve as it did in batch one. That argument for open weights is stronger than the price argument.

The counter-argument is operations. Someone has to serve the model. On GCP that is doable, but it is a cost the client rarely budgets for. I have seen more than one team pick an open model to save on tokens and then spend the savings on a platform engineer.

## Anthropic's round

Anthropic [raised $30B](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-raises-30-billion-series-g-funding-380-billion-post-money-valuation) at a $380B valuation on Thursday. I use Claude alongside Gemini for client work, and I care about the vendors I depend on staying solvent. That is the only lens I have on financing news. This round says Claude will be around. Good.

## Where I landed

I now run three model tiers in client systems. Gemini for anything on GCP where latency and integration matter. Claude for long-form reasoning and code. An open model for narrow, well-defined jobs that need to be pinned. That mix was not a strategy. It is what survived contact with real work. Each release this week made one of those tiers a little better. None of them changed the shape.
