I sat the Claude Certified Architect Foundations exam on Tuesday and passed. Anthropic opened the certification at its [Partner Summit](https://www.channeldive.com/news/anthropic-launches-claude-channel-partner-program/814569/) the week before, and I wanted to see what they think an architect should know. Short answer: less about prompts than I expected, more about tool contracts, context limits, and failure handling. That matches what breaks in our systems. The exam is scenario-based. You get a client situation and pick the design that holds up. I would have failed it two years ago and not because of the model knowledge.

## GTC and the hardware I do not buy

[NVIDIA held GTC](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/gtc-2026-news/) in San Jose from Monday. I skimmed the keynote. None of my clients buy GPUs. They rent inference from Google and the price per token is what I track, not the chip. The keynote still matters. Every price drop on Vertex over the past year followed a hardware generation. So I read GTC as a forecast of next year's inference bill.

## Small models are back on my list

[Mistral Small 4](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-small-4/) merges their reasoning, vision, and coding lines into one model under Apache 2.0. OpenAI followed with [GPT-5.4 mini and nano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-5.4) on Tuesday. Two releases in two days at the cheap end of the range. That is the end I care about for the Scout agents at Let's Forage. Nine agents run per session. Only two of them need a frontier model. The rest classify intent, format output, or pick a tool. A good small model there cuts cost per session more than any prompt trick. I am putting Small 4 into our eval harness next week against Gemini Flash on the routing tasks.

## What I make of it

The certification and the small models point the same way. The hard part of agent work is no longer the model. It is the shape of the system around it. Which agent gets which model, what each tool promises, and what happens when a call fails. That was true at Developers Inc. when the medical claims pipeline ran on a fine-tuned BERT. It is still true with a Mythos-class model in the loop.
