## What happened

- AWS made [AgentCore Evaluations generally available](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/03/agentcore-evaluations-generally-available) on Tuesday. Evaluation of agent runs is now a managed service on Bedrock, not a script you maintain.
- Claude Code moved [computer use into the CLI](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/whats-new/2026-w14) as a research preview. The agent can now drive a browser or a desktop app from the terminal.
- [Cursor 3](https://cursor.com/blog/cursor-3) added an Agents Window, a view for running several coding agents at once.

## What I make of it

The AWS release is the one I keep thinking about. We do not build on Bedrock. Our clients are on Google Cloud, and that is the whole reason Zazmic exists as a Premier Partner. But I have spent the better part of a year building what AWS just turned into a checkbox. Our eval harness runs LLM-as-judge over agent traces, scores retrieval with RAGAS, and blocks a prompt change in CI if the score drops. It took three engineers two months to make it trustworthy. Most of that time went into making the judge agree with a human more than eighty percent of the time.

A managed service does not remove that work. Someone still has to write the rubric and grade the calibration set. What it removes is the plumbing, and the plumbing is where small teams give up. I have watched two client teams skip evals entirely because the setup felt like a second project. If Google offers the same thing inside Agent Engine, and I expect they will, adoption on our side goes up. I would rather compete on rubrics than on plumbing.

Computer use in the Claude Code CLI is the other thread. I tried it on a form that one of our healthcare clients still fills by hand. It worked on the third try. Two failures were the agent misreading a dropdown. That is the same failure our document extraction pipeline had at Data Insight in 2021, on scanned forms instead of web forms. The models got better. The forms did not.
