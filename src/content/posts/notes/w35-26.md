A slow release week by 2026 standards. Three items, all from Anthropic, none of them a model.

## Memory with a control panel

Claude's memory now [works across chat and Cowork, and the user decides what is in it](https://claude.com/blog/claudes-memory-works-everywhere-and-you-decide-whats-in-it). The second half of that sentence is the product.

Agent memory has been the hardest design conversation with every client this year. Not the storage. The governance. Who can see what the agent remembers about a customer. Who can delete it. What happens when a planner leaves the company and the agent still holds her preferences. For Scout we built a memory store with an owner field and an expiry on every entry. It took two sprints. It should have been a platform feature. Now, at least for the consumer product, it is. I expect the enterprise API to follow and I will move our store onto it when it does.

## Chrome for everyone

[Claude in Chrome reached GA on all paid plans](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260827-claude-chrome-available/) on Wednesday. Last week I wrote about using browser-use for a supplier portal. This makes the same capability available to a non-developer. The planner could, in theory, do the Monday download with a browser agent herself. In practice her company's IT policy will decide that, not her. Policy is the bottleneck now. Not the model. Not the tool.

## A hardware standard

Anthropic opened a [Model Hardware Standard research preview](https://github.com/jqueryscript/anthropic-claude-timeline) and offered 10,000 free or discounted Team seats to scientists. I do not have a view on the hardware standard yet. I have a view on the seats. My MS thesis ran on a borrowed GPU and a lot of patience. A team seat with a frontier model would have changed what I could try in a semester.

## End of August

Twelve weekly notes since the Zazmic anniversary. Rereading them, the theme is not models. It is who decides. Adaptive thinking decides effort. Auto mode decides permission. Memory controls decide retention. The releases keep moving decisions from engineers to platforms to users. My job is to know which decisions our clients cannot afford to have made for them.
