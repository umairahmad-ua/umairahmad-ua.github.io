Three labs released models within 48 hours. I will take them in the order I care about them for client work.

## GPT-5.6 as a family

OpenAI released [GPT-5.6 as three models named Sol, Terra and Luna](https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-6/). The naming is new. The idea is not. A large model for hard reasoning, a middle model for most work, a small model for volume. Google has run this shape since Gemini 2.5. Anthropic has run it since Claude 3.

The interesting part for me is pricing tiers inside one release. Our cost-per-task dashboard for Scout shows that 70 percent of agent calls are routing and formatting. Those calls do not need the large model. They never did. A family release makes that argument easier to have with a client who asked for "the best model" in the contract.

## Grok 4.5

xAI put out [Grok 4.5](https://x.ai/news/grok-4-5) on Tuesday with a 1.5 trillion parameter base. I have no client on xAI and no eval run to report. Parameter count is not a result. I will run it when a client asks.

## Muse Spark 1.1

Meta released [Muse Spark 1.1](https://fortune.com/2026/07/09/meta-muse-spark-1-1-release-alexandr-wang-superintelligence-labs-mark-zuckerberg/). Muse Spark replaced Llama inside Meta AI in April. Meta is no longer the open-weights company it was two years ago. Qwen and DeepSeek hold that position now. That is a real change in where an enterprise looks when it wants weights it can host.

## The question I ask every new model

Before I read a benchmark table I ask one thing. Does it change a decision I made last quarter? For the migration agent, Sonnet 5's context window did. For the planning orchestrator, nothing this week did. The planners in Jordan do not care which model wrote the variance report. They care that the numbers reconcile to the ERP. That test has nothing to do with model releases and everything to do with our tool layer.

Three new models. Zero changed decisions. That is a fine week.
