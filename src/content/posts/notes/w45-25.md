## Kimi K2 Thinking

Moonshot released [Kimi K2 Thinking](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Nov/6/kimi-k2-thinking/) on Thursday. It is an open reasoning model that scores near the frontier labs on agentic benchmarks and can run hundreds of tool calls in sequence. I pulled it into our eval harness on Friday.

On the Scout research-assistant task it did well. Better than I expected on planning, slightly worse on following our output schema. Those two results together tell me something. The reasoning is there. The instruction-following polish is what the closed labs still sell. For an internal tool I would consider it. For a client-facing agent that has to produce a fixed report format, not this month.

## Compute everywhere

OpenAI signed a [38 billion dollar deal with AWS](https://openai.com/index/aws-and-openai-partnership/). Add that to the NVIDIA letter of intent from September and the Anthropic TPU deal from last month. Every frontier lab is now buying capacity from every cloud. For enterprise buyers this means model availability across clouds is going to improve. It also means the "which cloud" question and the "which model" question are separating. That is healthy.

## A lesson from the week

We had our first real incident on the migration agent. A translated procedure passed row-count reconciliation and still produced wrong totals. Counts matched. Sums did not. We now reconcile aggregates on every numeric column, not just counts. I mention this because it is the kind of check that no model release fixes. The agent did its job. The validation was too thin. Migration work is trust work, and trust is built one extra check at a time.

Next week I expect model news from OpenAI. The weekly cadence of releases is starting to feel like a schedule.
