This is the first of these notes. I have been at Zazmic for three months now, leading the engineers on agent systems for enterprise clients. Every week something changes in the tooling. I keep losing track of what mattered and what was noise. So I am writing it down, once a week, in a few hundred words.

## What happened

OpenAI released [GPT-5-Codex](https://openai.com/index/introducing-upgrades-to-codex/) on Monday. It is a version of GPT-5 tuned for long-running coding work. The interesting part is not the benchmark. It is that OpenAI now treats "agent that runs for hours" as a product category with its own model.

Anthropic quietly turned on [memory for Claude Team and Enterprise](https://www.axios.com/2025/10/23/anthropic-claude-memory-subscribers) plans the Friday before. Memory across sessions is the thing every client asks for in the first meeting. It is also the thing that breaks evaluation, because the same prompt no longer gives the same answer.

## What I make of it

Both releases point the same direction. The unit of work is moving from a single completion to a session that persists. That changes what I have to test. On the Scout agents for Let's Forage we spent most of this week on session memory, not on prompts. Where does state live, who can read it, and what happens when a client asks us to delete it. None of that is a model question.

The model vendors are releasing features faster than the eval tooling catches up. My rule for the team stays the same. If we cannot replay a session and score it, the feature does not go to the client. That rule cost us two days this week. It will save us a month later.
