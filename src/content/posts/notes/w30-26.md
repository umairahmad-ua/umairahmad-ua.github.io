## Opus 5

Anthropic released [Claude Opus 5](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/whats-new/2026-w30) on Friday. A million tokens of context, 128K tokens of output, and adaptive thinking on by default. The third item is the one that changes our code.

For a year we have carried a thinking budget parameter through every agent config. Low for routing, high for the migration validator, none for formatting. The model now decides how long to think per request. That removes a knob I tuned by hand and it removes a knob I could explain to a client. Both are true. I am running the operations agent with adaptive thinking this week and watching two numbers. Task success rate and cost per task. If both hold, the knob goes.

The 128K output limit matters for one workflow. The migration documentation agent writes lineage for a whole schema in one pass. It used to stop at 32K and we resumed with a continuation prompt. Continuation prompts are where formatting drift lives. One pass is cleaner.

## Two Flash models

Google released [Gemini 3.6 Flash and Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_(language_model)) on Tuesday. Scout runs on Flash for every agent except the two authoring agents. The Flash line is where our volume is. A better Flash model at the same price is worth more to our unit economics than any frontier release this year. I moved the research assistant agent to 3.6 Flash on Thursday. The eval set passed. Groundedness held. Latency dropped a little.

## What I think about defaults

Both releases move decisions from the engineer to the platform. Adaptive thinking decides effort. A cheaper Flash decides where volume goes. I welcome this and I distrust it in the same breath. A default is a decision someone else made for a median customer. Our clients are not median. The eval harness is how we find out where the default is wrong for us. Without it, a default is a guess with a brand name on it.
