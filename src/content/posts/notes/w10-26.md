OpenAI had a week. Google had a Tuesday.

## What happened

- Google released [Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_(language_model)) on Tuesday.
- OpenAI released [GPT-5.3 Instant](https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-3-instant/) the same day.
- The [Codex app came to Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI_Codex_(AI_agent)) on Wednesday.
- OpenAI released [GPT-5.4 Thinking and Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-5.4) on Thursday.

## The one I care about

Flash-Lite. Not because it is the smartest model of the week. Because it is the cheapest one on the platform I build on. The Scout system makes a lot of small calls: classify an intent, extract a field, decide whether to hand off. Those calls do not need reasoning. They need to be right, fast, and cheap. I moved the intent classifier to Flash-Lite on Thursday and reran the suite. Accuracy held. Cost per session dropped by a visible amount. That is a good Thursday.

## On the OpenAI cadence

Three models in a week is a lot to absorb. I do not think most enterprise buyers can. When a client asks me which OpenAI model to use, the honest answer is that the one they picked last quarter is probably still fine. Model releases are now faster than procurement cycles. The vendor that makes upgrading boring will win the enterprise, and boring is not what this week felt like.

## Migration update

Batch seven cleared review on Friday. That puts us past the halfway point. The finance analyst who caught the join bug in January now runs the reconciliation reports herself. That was the goal. An agent the client can operate is worth more than an agent I have to babysit. Cutover for the first production workload is planned for late March. I will report what breaks.
